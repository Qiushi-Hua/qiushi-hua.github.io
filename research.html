Monetary Policy and Firm Innovation

Job market paper · JEL E52, O31, O32, O34

I build a firm-level panel that joins Compustat, USPTO and CRSP, then estimate the dynamic response to monetary policy shocks with local projections. Almost the entire R&D response runs through the intensive margin. An expansionary shock raises R&D spending and knowledge capital at firms that already innovate, while the rest barely move.

On the output side, easing lowers patent counts but raises average citations per patent, and over the medium run it raises patent importance and the share of high-impact patents. A two-stage analysis shows this reallocation works through the R&D response itself rather than through a direct effect of the shock on patenting. Contractionary shocks are not the mirror image, since they cut both the quantity and the quality of innovation. Rates move the direction of innovation, not only its scale.

Fixed Costs in Lumpy Investment Models

Work in progress

A dynamic structural model of heterogeneous firms investing under capital adjustment frictions and incomplete information, where each firm sees only a noisy signal of its own productivity. I solve the stochastic dynamic program by value function iteration in Julia and Matlab and use forward and backward iteration checks to keep the solution numerically stable. A Monte Carlo framework then traces transition dynamics and policy effects after aggregate shocks, with robustness over a parameter grid and alternative policy scenarios.

Labour Market and Skill Demand in AI Job Postings

Applied data project

An NLP and machine learning pipeline over millions of online job postings. Text preprocessing, semantic feature extraction, supervised classification and topic modelling feed a set of interactive dashboards that track how employer demand for AI-related skills shifts across industries and regions.
