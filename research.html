Monetary Policy and Firm Innovation

JEL E52, O31, O32, O34

This paper studies how monetary policy affects firm innovation. I link Compustat, USPTO and CRSP into a firm-level panel and estimate dynamic responses to monetary policy shocks using local projections.

The R&D response operates almost entirely on the intensive margin. An expansionary shock raises R&D spending and knowledge capital among firms that already innovate. Firms that do not already innovate respond little.

Patent counts fall after an expansionary shock while average citations per patent rise. Over the medium run, patent importance and the share of high-impact patents both increase. A two-stage analysis attributes the shift to the R&D response itself rather than to a direct effect of the shock on patenting. Contractionary shocks reduce innovation on both margins, quantity as well as quality.

Fixed Costs in Lumpy Investment Models

Work in progress

A model of heterogeneous firms investing under capital adjustment costs, where each firm observes only a noisy signal of its own productivity. I solve the dynamic program by value function iteration in Julia and Matlab, then simulate transition paths after an aggregate shock. Calibration targets observed investment patterns. I check the results across a grid of parameters.

Labour Market and Skill Demand in AI Job Postings

Applied data project

I built a pipeline that reads several million online job postings, classifies the roles and extracts the skills each one asks for. Topic modelling and a supervised classifier do the text work. The output is a dashboard that tracks employer demand by industry and region.
