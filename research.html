Monetary Policy and Firm Innovation

JEL E52, O31, O32, O34

This paper asks how monetary policy affects firm innovation. I link Compustat, USPTO and CRSP into a firm-level panel and estimate the response to monetary policy shocks with local projections. Almost all of the R&D response runs through the intensive margin. An expansionary shock raises R&D spending and knowledge capital at firms that already innovate and leaves the rest largely unmoved. The same shock lowers patent counts but raises average citations per patent, and over the medium run it raises patent importance and the share of high-impact patents. A two-stage analysis attributes that shift to the R&D response itself rather than to a direct effect of the shock on patenting. Contractionary shocks cut both the quantity and the quality of innovation.

Fixed Costs in Lumpy Investment Models

Work in progress

A model of heterogeneous firms investing under capital adjustment costs, where each firm observes only a noisy signal of its own productivity. I solve the dynamic program by value function iteration in Julia and Matlab, then simulate transition paths after an aggregate shock. I calibrate to observed investment patterns and check the results across a grid of parameters.

Labour Market and Skill Demand in AI Job Postings

Applied data project

I built a pipeline that reads several million online job postings, classifies the roles and extracts the skills each one asks for. Topic modelling and a supervised classifier do the text work. The output is a dashboard that tracks employer demand by industry and region.
