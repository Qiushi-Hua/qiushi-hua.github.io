Teaching

I am the instructor of record for ECO 4213 Monetary Economics at the University of Florida. I wrote the syllabus, the slides, the problem sets and both exams. I have also assisted ten courses here since 2022, from intermediate undergraduate classes up to the doctoral micro core. My teaching statement says more about how I teach and what I am ready to take on.

ECO 4213  Monetary Economics

A 4000-level course on monetary policy. The first half covers central banks, the money supply process and how interest rates get set. The tools come next, the ordinary rate changes and the unconventional ones like quantitative easing and forward guidance. The second half is model building. Students derive the IS curve, the monetary policy curve and the aggregate demand curve, then put them to work on the Great Depression, 2008 and the COVID-19 response.

Running the course also means enrollment, accommodation letters and academic integrity paperwork.

Course materials

  • The syllabus and a schedule of 23 lectures.
  • Fourteen chapters of beamer slides, written from scratch, each with a speaker script.
  • The opening chapter’s figures, which I draw in matplotlib from FRED data instead of reusing the textbook plots.
  • Four problem sets with full answer keys. Each has multiple choice, short answer and calculation sections.
  • Five short in-class exercises. They are not announced in advance, so attendance matters.
  • A group project for teams of four or five, with two presentations and a written report. Teammates rate each other afterwards.
  • Both exams, which I write and grade.

Assessment

  • Four problem sets, 5% each.
  • Five in-class exercises, 2% each.
  • Group project, 25%.
  • Midterm 20%, final 25%.
