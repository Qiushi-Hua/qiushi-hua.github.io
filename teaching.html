Teaching

In the Classroom

I am the instructor of record for ECO 4213 Monetary Economics at the University of Florida. The syllabus, the lecture notes, the problem sets, the group project and both exams are mine. Before that I spent two years as a teaching assistant in intermediate macroeconomics.

ECO 4213  Monetary Economics

A 4000-level course on how monetary policy shapes the wider economy. We work through the structure of central banks, the money supply process and the determination of interest rates, then move to the tools themselves, both the conventional short-rate adjustments and the unconventional ones such as quantitative easing and forward guidance.

The second half is model building. Students derive the IS curve, the monetary policy and aggregate demand curves, and aggregate demand and supply analysis, then use them on the Great Depression, the 2008 financial crisis and the COVID-19 response.

What I built for it

  • Wrote the syllabus, the schedule of 23 lectures and the whole grading policy.
  • Wrote every lecture deck myself in LaTeX beamer, 14 chapters, each with a full speaker script.
  • Built the opening macro figures in matplotlib straight from FRED series instead of reusing textbook scans.
  • Wrote four problem sets with complete answer keys, mixing multiple choice, short answer and calculation questions.
  • Designed five unannounced in-class exercises so students practise the modelling before it counts on an exam.
  • Set up a group project for teams of four or five, with two in-class presentations, a written report, and peer evaluation to catch free riding.
  • Wrote and graded the midterm and the final.
  • Handle enrolment, accommodation letters and academic integrity documentation.

How the grade is split

  • Four problem sets, 5% each.
  • Five in-class exercises, 2% each.
  • Group project, 25%.
  • Midterm, 20%. Final, 25%.
