Teaching

I am the instructor of record for ECO 4213 Monetary Economics at the University of Florida. I wrote the syllabus, the slides, the problem sets and both exams. Before that I spent two years as a teaching assistant in intermediate macroeconomics.

ECO 4213  Monetary Economics

A 4000-level course on monetary policy. The first half covers central banks, the money supply process and how interest rates get set, then the tools themselves, ordinary rate changes alongside quantitative easing and forward guidance. The second half is model building. Students derive the IS curve, the monetary policy curve and the aggregate demand curve, then put them to work on the Great Depression, 2008 and the COVID-19 response.

Running the course also means enrolment, accommodation letters and academic integrity paperwork.

Course materials

  • The syllabus and a schedule of 23 lectures.
  • Fourteen chapters of beamer slides, written from scratch, each with a speaker script.
  • The opening chapter's figures, which I draw in matplotlib from FRED series rather than scan out of the textbook.
  • Four problem sets with full answer keys. Each has multiple choice, short answer and calculation sections.
  • Five short in-class exercises. They are not announced in advance, so attendance matters.
  • A group project for teams of four or five, with two presentations and a written report. Teammates rate each other afterwards.
  • Both exams, which I write and grade.

Assessment

  • Four problem sets, 5% each.
  • Five in-class exercises, 2% each.
  • Group project, 25%.
  • Midterm 20%, final 25%.
